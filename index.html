Opening paragraphWhen Worcester and Malvern homeowners start thinking about extending their home, one of the first decisions to make is whether to build single storey or double storey. Both are popular, both have their advantages, and the right choice depends entirely on what you need, what your budget allows, and what your plot can accommodate. H2 — Section 2Double Storey Extensions — Pros and Cons
Section 2 bodyA double storey extension adds floor area on both ground and first floor levels. It is the more substantial project, typically adding a large room downstairs — often kitchen or living space — with one or two bedrooms and potentially a bathroom above. The main advantage of double storey is the sheer amount of space added for the investment. Because foundations, external walls and roof are largely shared costs between the two floors, the cost per square metre is significantly lower than building two separate single storey extensions. For families who need more living space and more bedrooms, a double storey extension solves both requirements in a single project. The trade-offs are cost, time and planning. Double storey extensions always require full planning permission — there is no Permitted Development route — which adds both time and cost to the project. They are more structurally complex, take longer to build (typically 16 to 24 weeks), and cause more disruption to the household during the build period. The total cost is also higher, even if the cost per square metre is better value.
H2 — Section 3Which Type of Extension Adds More Value?
Section 3 bodyIn general, double storey extensions add more absolute value to a property because they add more floor area — and in the Worcestershire property market, additional bedrooms command a meaningful premium. A three-bedroom house in Worcester or Malvern is worth considerably more with an additional bedroom added, and a double storey extension is the most cost-effective way to achieve that. Single storey extensions also add value, but the return on investment varies more depending on the property and the specification. A high quality open plan kitchen-diner extension on a property in a good Worcestershire location will typically return more than its cost in added value. A more modest addition may add less. The honest answer is that both types of extension add value when properly designed and well built. The specific return depends on the property, the area, the specification and the local market at the time of sale.
H2 — Section 4Questions to Ask Before Choosing
Section 4 bodyBefore deciding between single and double storey, it is worth being clear on a few things: What do you actually need the space for? If the answer is more bedrooms, double storey is almost certainly the right choice. If the answer is a better kitchen or more living space, single storey may be sufficient. What is your total budget? Double storey costs more in absolute terms. If budget is constrained, a well-specified single storey extension is better than a compromised double storey. How long can you manage the disruption? A double storey build is a longer, more disruptive project. For families with young children or complex schedules, this is a real practical consideration. Is your garden large enough? Both types reduce garden space. A double storey extension has a smaller footprint than two separate single storey builds of the same total floor area, but the shadow and visual impact is greater.
